# Strange Ranger: Phase 3 Build Spec

Phase 3 brings people into the park. Every site the ranger has made ready starts to draw visitors, who walk the ranger's trails, pay their way, make a mess, and get into gentle trouble. It was written before the designer's play tests of phases 2a and 2b and should be revised against them before the lab starts.

## Goal and scope

Phase 3 must prove that the ranger's work turns into a living park: that opening a place visibly fills it with people, that the park quietly pays its own way, and that ignoring it is funny, never ruinous.

The scene that proves it: the ranger comes back from three days in the backcountry to find hikers on the trail they built, a scout troop camped where there was only grass, forty dollars in the till, a note in the suggestion box asking for a bench at the outlook, a grumbling letter from the Chief about the bathrooms, and a troll badger asleep in one of them.

**In phase 3:**

- Money: a wallet, a ledger, a weekly budget from the Chief, and a supply catalog in place of the free supply shelf.
- Visitors as small characters who arrive at the gate, follow trails and signs to ready sites, and go home.
- Six kinds of visitor, scout troops among them.
- Visitors who get lost, and the ranger finding them.
- Facilities built at set places: bathrooms, picnic areas, campgrounds, a fishing dock.
- Small things placed freely: benches, trash cans, picnic tables, fire rings.
- Renting out the old cabin.
- Income from entrance fees, camping, tours, fishing permits, gear rental, cabin rental and a gift shelf.
- Five gift shelf goods the ranger can make.
- Upkeep: bathrooms to clean, trash to empty, litter to pick up.
- Troll badgers, drawn by mess.
- Hired staff who take over upkeep.
- A suggestion box.
- The Chief's weekly report and his notes.

**Not in phase 3:** the town, townsfolk, the outfitter, creatures other than troll badgers, the choice of making a magical site public, cryptid hunters and photographers, park status, the yearly review, the cart, boats, setting prices. Troll badgers here are a source of mischief only; befriending them and understanding them waits for phase 5.

**Carried over.** Everything in the earlier specs still holds, including the secrecy rules. Where this spec needs to say something about a place the designer has not been told of, it says it in the spoiler addendum instead.

## Money

The park now runs on dollars. Money gives exploring a payoff and the ranger something to spend on, but the player can never be stuck for lack of it.

**The basics.**

- The wallet shows on screen beside the clock.
- Every amount is in whole dollars.
- Prices are fixed, as in Stardew. The ranger does not set fees or prices.
- A **Ledger** page joins the job list and notebook. It shows today's income and spending by source, and a running total for this week and last.

**The Chief's budget.**

- A new game, or an older save loaded into phase 3, starts with $500.
- Every seventh day, the park receives $250 from the Chief's budget. This is the guarantee that the player is never stuck: a week of doing nothing still buys a cabin's worth of nails.
- The budget never grows or shrinks with how the park is doing.

**The supply catalog.** The free supply shelf is replaced by a catalog on the station desk.

- Anything ordered by midnight arrives on the station's supply shelf at 6:00 the next morning.
- Facility kits are the exception: they are left at their pad, as described under Facilities.
- The catalog lists supplies, facility kits and small placeable things, each with its price.

| Supply | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Nails (bag of 10) | $15 |
| Plank | $8 |
| Roofing | $10 |
| Window glass | $25 |
| Firewood (bundle of 5) | $10 |
| Blank sign | $15 |
| Radio valve | $60 |
| Tin of beans | $6 |
| Sandwich | $8 |

Planks, roofing, firewood and signs can still be made at no cost from what the ranger gathers, so a frugal ranger spends little.

**The cost of a rescue.** A collapse now costs 10% of the money in the wallet, up to $250, taken as a fee for whoever brought the ranger back. The rescue message mentions it in one gentle line. With an empty wallet, it costs nothing.

## Visitors

Visitors are small characters who come to the park for the day or for a few nights. They go only where the ranger has made ready and joined by trail, so the crowd spreads visibly across the map as the park grows.

### Kinds of visitor

| Kind | Party | Stays | Wants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Day hiker | 1 to 2 | One day | A ready site to walk to, a bench on the way |
| Family | 3 to 5 | One day | A picnic area, a bathroom, a short walk |
| Campers | 2 to 4 | 1 to 3 nights | A campground, a bathroom, sites within walking distance of camp |
| Anglers | 1 to 2 | One day, or with campers | A fishing dock |
| Tour group | 6 to 10, with a guide | One day | A string of ready sites |
| Scout troop | 6 to 8 scouts and a leader | 2 nights | A campground, then things to do: visiting sites, collecting litter for a badge, a campfire sing-along |

Scouts are the lively ones. They walk in a line, wave at the ranger, occasionally wander off, and leave drawings in the suggestion box.

### How visitors decide where to go

- Visitors only walk on trail, stone steps and boardwalk. They never go cross-country.
- They only visit sites that are ready, as defined in phase 2a.
- Each party chooses a destination it can reach and return from within its trip:
  - A day party arrives between 8:00 and 11:00 and must be back at the gate by 18:00, or by dusk if earlier. A site farther than that is out of its reach.
  - Campers measure their reach from their campground, not the gate. Building a campground is therefore how far sites are opened to the public.
- At a junction, a party follows a pointer sign to its destination if there is one. With no sign, it takes the shortest trail it knows.
- Visitors walk a little slower than the ranger and stop now and then: at benches, views, signs and finds.
- Bad weather sends parties home early or into the nearest shelter. Campers in a storm stay in their tents.

### How many come

- Each morning the game works out the day's arrivals from the park's appeal, the season, the weather and the day of the week.
- **Appeal** comes from each ready site, raised by its facilities and lowered by mess at it.
- Summer is busiest, spring and autumn half as busy, winter a quarter. Days six and seven of each week are twice as busy. Rain halves arrivals; storms and blizzards nearly stop them.
- A park with no ready site gets no visitors.

### Moods

Each party keeps a simple score through its visit: up for reaching its destination, a clean bathroom when needed, a bench, a picnic table, a nice find; down for getting lost, a dirty bathroom, nowhere to put trash, or badger trouble. The week's average mood is shown in the Chief's report and gently raises or lowers the next week's arrivals.

### Lost visitors

- In fog, at night or in a storm, a party at a junction with no pointer toward its destination may take a wrong turn and become lost. At most one party is lost at a time.
- The ranger learns of it on the radio at the station or a tower, or from a note on the station board. A job is added with a rough rumor circle around where they were last seen.
- When the ranger reaches them and talks to them, they follow the ranger to the nearest trail and make their own way back.
- Finding them earns a thank-you card in the suggestion box, a tip of $20 to $50, and 60 Surveying experience.
- A party nobody finds turns up at the gate at 6:00 the next morning, sheepish and unharmed. Nothing bad ever happens to a visitor.
- A completed site map and good pointer signs make visitors much less likely to get lost.

### How visitors are simulated

- A party near the ranger, in loaded chunks, is a full character with walking, animation and behavior.
- Everywhere else, a party is a cheap record moving along the trail network at walking speed. It becomes a full character smoothly when the ranger draws near.
- At most 80 visitors are in the park at once. Arrivals beyond that wait for tomorrow.
- Every visitor's income, mess and mood is counted whether or not the ranger can see them.

## Facilities

The bible's middle option holds: large facilities go at set places, small things go anywhere sensible, and every player's park ends up looking like their own.

### Pads: the set places

- A **pad** is a marked patch of ground where one particular facility can be built. It appears, as a few survey stakes with a flag, once its site is ready.
- Each pad takes one kind of facility.
- Pads for the gate, the old cabin and the outlook are listed below. Pads for other sites are in the spoiler addendum, and the designer discovers them in play.

| Place | Pads |
| --- | --- |
| The gate | Visitor kiosk (already built), bathroom, picnic area |
| The old cabin | Picnic area |
| The outlook | Picnic area |

### Building a facility

1. Order its kit from the catalog.
2. The next morning the kit is waiting at its pad as a stack of crates.
3. The ranger goes there and builds it with the hammer, like a cabin repair, in the number of actions listed. Each action takes 20 game minutes.

| Facility | Kit price | Hammer actions | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bathroom | $400 | 6 | Visitors use it. Needs cleaning. |
| Picnic area | $150 | 3 | Two tables. Families come for it. Raises a site's appeal. |
| Campground | $800 | 9 | Six tent spots and a fire ring. Campers and scouts stay here and measure their reach from here. |
| Fishing dock | $300 | 4 | Anglers come for it. Sells fishing permits. |
| Tower lookout | $200 | 3 | A rail and a visitors' sign at a repaired fire tower. Visitors may climb it by day. The ranger's room, bed and chest stay private. |

A built facility can be taken down with the hammer, refunding half its price.

### Small things placed freely

Benches, trash cans, picnic tables and fire rings can be placed on open ground anywhere within 6 tiles of a trail, on ground the ranger has revealed, and never on the trail itself.

| Thing | Catalog price | Can be made | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bench | $30 | At a workbench: 3 planks, 4 nails | Visitors rest here, and their mood rises |
| Trash can | $25 | No | Visitors put trash in it. Holds 20. Needs emptying. |
| Picnic table | $40 | At a workbench: 4 planks, 6 nails | Families stop and eat |
| Fire ring | $20 | Anywhere: 6 stones | Campers gather round it. The ranger can light it. |

Placed things are picked up again whole by interacting with the hammer.

### Renting out the old cabin

- A small sign on the cabin door can be set to **For rent** or **Ranger only**. It starts as Ranger only.
- When set to For rent, campers may book it for a night. Bookings are announced in the morning, at the station, so the ranger can plan.
- On a booked night, the cabin's bed and chest are not the ranger's to use; a note on the door says who has it. The ranger can always camp outside.
- A night's rent is $40.
- Renters count as campers and measure their reach from the cabin. A rented cabin opens the land beyond it to the public, much as a campground would.
- Fire towers are never rented.

## Income

The ranger never collects money by hand. Every source runs by itself once its place is open, and income arrives in the wallet as it is earned.

**The visitor kiosk.** A small hut at the gate, already standing when phase 3 begins, put up by the Chief. It takes entrance fees, rents gear, books campsites and the cabin, sells park maps, holds the gift shelf and has the suggestion box beside it. It is staffed by nobody in particular and needs no attention.

| Source | Price | Earned when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entrance fee | $5 a visitor | Each visitor passes the gate |
| Camping permit | $15 a party a night | A party spends a night at a campground |
| Cabin rent | $40 a night | The cabin is booked |
| Tour | $12 a person | A tour group sets out |
| Fishing permit | $6 an angler | An angler fishes at a dock |
| Gear rental | $5 a visitor | About one visitor in five rents a lantern or a bedroll |
| Park map | $8 | Visitors buy one; on sale once the ranger has mapped at least three sites |
| Gift shelf | Varies | Visitors buy what the ranger has stocked |

**Tours.**

- Tour groups form only when at least two ready sites lie within a day's reach of the gate, or of a campground.
- A group walks a loop through every ready site in reach, guided by a hired guide who is part of the group and needs nothing from the ranger.
- Each ready site in the loop raises how often tours run.

**Fishing.** Anglers fish catch-and-release from a dock. The ranger never fishes, as the bible says. Anglers sometimes leave a thank-you note describing something odd they saw in the water.

**The gift shelf.**

- The kiosk has a shelf of 12 slots. The ranger stocks it by interacting with it and choosing items from the backpack.
- Visitors buy from it at random as they leave, about one in four, choosing among what is in stock.
- The ranger can stock any gift good, and also berry preserve, which sells for $15.
- Phase 3 adds five gift goods. Their recipes are learned as an idea the first time the ranger looks at the empty shelf.

| Good | Made | Needs | Sells for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pressed flower card | Anywhere | 3 wildflowers | $10 |
| Pine resin candle | Workbench | 2 pine resin, 1 stick | $12 |
| Carved pinecone figure | Workbench | 1 plank, 1 pine resin | $18 |
| Painted stone | Anywhere | 2 stones, 1 berries | $8 |
| Glowcap night-light | Workbench | 1 glowcap, 1 window glass, 1 plank | $60 |

The night-light uses a magical ingredient, so selling it means not keeping it for a refit. That trade is meant.

## Upkeep, neglect and troll badgers

Visitors make a mess. Clearing it up is a small, satisfying chore, and leaving it is never a disaster: it brings grumbles from the Chief, glum suggestion cards, and troll badgers.

### Upkeep

| Chore | How | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean a bathroom | Interact with it | 20 game minutes, 10 energy. Restores it to spotless. |
| Empty a trash can | Interact with it | 10 game minutes, 4 energy |
| Pick up litter | Interact with it, like forage | No time, 1 energy. Occasionally turns up a lost visitor belonging, logged in the notebook. |

- A bathroom's cleanliness runs from 100 down to 0. Each visit lowers it by 4. Below 40 it is grubby and visitors grumble. Below 15 it is filthy and visitors avoid it.
- A trash can holds 20. A full one takes no more.
- A visitor with trash and no can with room within 15 tiles has an even chance of dropping it as litter on or beside the trail.
- Scout troops on a litter badge pick some up for the ranger.

### Mess

Each site has a **mess level** made from its bathrooms, its trash cans and the litter within its area. Mess lowers the site's appeal and visitors' moods. A site with no facilities and no litter has no mess.

### Troll badgers

Troll badgers are small, round, grumpy and hungry, and mess draws them.

- Once a site has been messy for two nights, each night brings an even chance that a troll badger moves in.
- A badger at a site gets up to one bit of mischief each day:
  - tipping over a trash can, spilling its contents as six pieces of litter;
  - napping in a bathroom, which closes it until the badger is moved;
  - raiding a picnic, which annoys a family and pleases the badger;
  - digging in the trail, which turns one trail tile to dug earth and may break the site's trail step until the ranger fills it in.
- The ranger can shoo a badger by interacting with it. It takes 10 game minutes. The badger waddles off, grumbling, and is back the next day if the mess remains.
- When the site's mess is cleared, its badger leaves for good, until the next time.
- No site holds more than one badger, and no more than three are in the park at once.
- The first badger the ranger meets is logged in the notebook.
- Badgers are never harmed, never caught and never fed in phase 3. They never move signs.

## Staff, the suggestion box and the Chief

Three things keep the ranger in touch with a park that runs while they are away: staff who take chores off their hands, a box of visitors' opinions, and the Chief's letters.

### Staff

Hiring staff is how chores fade over time, as sprinklers do in Stardew.

- A staff board in the station lists people looking for seasonal work, two or three at a time, each with a name and one line about them.
- Hiring is done at the board. Wages are paid every seventh day, after the Chief's budget arrives.
- If the wallet cannot cover the wages, the staff take that week off. There is no debt, and nobody quits.
- Staff are full characters when near the ranger and simple records elsewhere, like visitors. They live off-site and arrive each morning.
- Letting someone go is done at the board, with a kind line from them.

| Role | Wage | Does each day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Custodian | $100 a week | Cleans up to 4 assigned bathrooms or trash cans |
| Trail crew | $140 a week | Picks up litter along trails, fills in badger diggings, and clears one storm-fallen trunk |

- More than one of each can be hired. The ranger assigns custodians to facilities at the board.
- Staff do not shoo badgers. They clear the mess, and the badger then leaves on its own.

### The suggestion box

- The box beside the kiosk fills each night with cards from that day's visitors, up to five a day.
- The ranger reads them by interacting with the box. Read cards are kept in a **Suggestions** page in the notebook.
- Cards are written from what really happened in the park: a complaint about a particular bathroom, thanks for a bench, a request for a picnic area at a named site, a child's drawing of a badger, a thank-you from a found party.
- Each card is a few words in a visitor's own voice. Some are funny, some are sweet, none is an order.
- Cards that ask for something are marked, and the job list can show them as invitations if the ranger chooses to pin them.

### The Chief

The Chief now writes regularly, always to the station's note board, always warm.

- **The weekly report.** Every seventh morning: visitors that week, income and spending by source, the visitors' mood, the week's best suggestion card, and one line from the Chief.
- **Grumbles.** If a bathroom has been filthy for three days, or badgers have been active on three nights in a week, the Chief sends a short, kind, slightly pained note about it. At most one grumble a week.
- **Praise.** Short notes for milestones: the first visitors, the first campers, the first $500 week, the first lost party found, the first week with every bathroom clean.
- **Hints.** About one letter in four ends with a line that suggests he knows more about the park than he says. These are written by the designer or the lab and approved by the designer, and never explain anything.
- He never visits, never threatens, and never sets a target.

## Build order, art and tests

Phase 3 is the largest phase yet, built in ten steps, each ending with passing tests and a spoiler-free status email. If it proves too large, the lab proposes splitting it after step 5, as the roadmap allows.

### Build order

1. **Money.** Wallet, ledger, the Chief's budget, the catalog and next-morning delivery, the rescue fee, and loading older saves with $500.
2. **The trail network.** A graph of every trail, step and boardwalk tile, kept up to date as trail is dug, cleared or broken, with travel times between points. Visitors depend on it.
3. **Visitors, simple.** Day hikers only: arriving, choosing a reachable ready site, walking there and back, the far-away record and the near-by character, and the 80-visitor cap.
4. **Facilities.** Pads, kits, building, taking down, small placed things, the kiosk, the cabin rental sign.
5. **Income.** Every source in the table, the gift shelf and its goods.
6. **All visitor kinds.** Families, campers, anglers, tour groups and scouts; reach measured from campgrounds and the rented cabin; weather behavior; moods; arrivals from appeal.
7. **Upkeep and mess.** Bathrooms, trash, litter, lost belongings, mess levels.
8. **Troll badgers.**
9. **Staff, cards and letters.** The staff board and both roles, the suggestion box, the weekly report, grumbles, praise and hints. Lost visitors are built here too, because they rely on the radio and the job list.
10. **Full pass.** All tests, a played month of game time by the lab, and a final report.

### Debug tools

The panel gains: add or remove money; skip to the seventh day; spawn a party of any kind; set a bathroom's cleanliness or a can's contents; send a badger to a site; lose a party; show every visitor on the map; and run the park unattended for a chosen number of days and report what happened.

### Art

Phase 3 needs many characters, which image generators find hardest. The lab should ask for one plain visitor body in four directions and four walking frames, plus separate layers for clothes, hats and packs that it combines to make many visitors from a few pictures. The art request is split in two: Art Request 04 for visitors, staff and the troll badger; Art Request 05 for facilities, placed things, litter, the kiosk, the gift goods and the new screens.

### Automated tests

| Area | Must be true |
| --- | --- |
| Money | The budget arrives every seventh day; orders arrive next morning; the rescue fee is 10% up to $250 and nothing from an empty wallet; the ledger totals match the wallet |
| Trail network | It updates within the same frame as any change to trail; travel times on it match walked times within 5% |
| Reach | No visitor ever stands on ground that is not trail; no visitor visits a site that is not ready; a day party never chooses a site it cannot return from in time; a campground or rented cabin extends reach as specified |
| Arrivals | Over a simulated year, arrivals follow the season, day and weather multipliers within 5%; a park with no ready site gets none; the cap is never exceeded |
| Near and far | A party switching between record and character keeps its position, plans, trash and mood |
| Facilities | Each kit arrives at its pad; each facility needs its hammer actions; refunds are correct; placed things obey the distance and ground rules |
| Income | Every source earns its price under its condition and only then; the gift shelf sells only what is stocked |
| Rental | A booked cabin's bed and chest are unusable that night and usable the next; bookings are announced in the morning |
| Upkeep | Cleanliness, can contents and litter change as tuned; each chore restores what it says |
| Badgers | They arrive only after the tuned nights of mess; each mischief does what its row says; shooing works for a day; clearing mess removes them; never more than the tuned number; they never touch a sign |
| Lost visitors | A lost party is announced, found and returns as specified; an unfound party is at the gate by 6:00; at most one is lost at a time; no visitor ever comes to harm |
| Staff | Each role does its daily work; unpaid staff take the week off and return; no debt is ever recorded |
| Cards and letters | Every card and letter is triggered only by its event; grumbles never exceed one a week |
| Unattended park | A save left for 28 days of debug time with no ranger action ends with no failure state and the wallet not below zero |
| Performance | 60 frames per second with 80 visitors in the park and 30 of them on screen |
| Saves | A phase 2b save loads; all new state, including every visitor and badger, saves and loads exactly |
| Secrecy | No status email, commit message or debug screen names a spoiler pad or site unless "show spoilers" is ticked |

### Play checklist

- [ ] Make a site ready, and watch the first visitors walk to it on your trail.
- [ ] Build a bathroom and a picnic area, and place benches and trash cans where you think they belong.
- [ ] Build a campground, and see visitors reach places a day hiker could not.
- [ ] Rent out the cabin, and plan a trip around a booking.
- [ ] Stock the gift shelf with things you made, and see them sell.
- [ ] Read a week of suggestion cards, and act on one you like.
- [ ] Find a lost party in fog.
- [ ] Leave the park for a week of backcountry, come back to the mess, and judge whether it is funny or annoying.
- [ ] Meet a troll badger, and shoo it.
- [ ] Hire a custodian, and judge whether the chores fading feels like a reward.
- [ ] Read the Chief's weekly report, and judge whether he sounds like himself.
- [ ] Judge whether money feels too tight, too loose or about right.
- [ ] Write down what felt wrong, and what you wanted to build and could not.
