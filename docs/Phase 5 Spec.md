# Strange Ranger: Phase 5 Build Spec

From the lab, 25 November 2026, for the designer to change as he likes. Written from the bible, the open questions list, and what the Phase 4 play tests taught.

Phase 5 is when the park comes alive at the edges of your eye. Rabbits in the meadow, a deer at the tree line at dusk, and now and then something that is nearly a rabbit. It adds the animals and their strange cousins, binoculars, a camera and the field guide, the Lore skill, a pet, the night as a place of its own, the full moon, aurora nights, and the first magical creature the ranger can meet, help and decide about.

## Goal and scope

Phase 5 must prove that the strange side of the park is worth going out for: that watching, photographing and slowly befriending odd creatures is as good a reason to leave home as fixing a trail or drinking coffee.

The scene that proves it: on the night of the full moon the ranger walks out with the dog, past a field of rabbits (one has antlers), to the meadow where a moose has been standing about all month looking self-conscious. Tonight there's a tall, awkward man there instead, holding his own elbows. He would very much like to try a coffee in town, but he doesn't know how you order one. The ranger helps over the next few full moons, then decides whether anyone else ever hears about him.

**Phase 5 is built in two halves,** as Phase 4 was.

| Half | Adds | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5a: Out in the woods | Ordinary animals, binoculars and the camera, the field guide, the Lore skill, the first strange cousins, the night as its own world, a pet | A player can spend an evening watching and photographing animals, find their first strange cousin, and wants to fill the guide |
| 5b: Gord and the night | Creature friendships and their answers (befriend, what it wants, a deal, coaxing it to move), Gord, the secret, hint or public choice, night magic, full moons and calendar events, aurora nights | A player can meet, settle and decide the fate of one magical creature |

**Not in Phase 5:** new regions; the sites of Phase 6 (the Sprite Market, Troll Badger Bridge's toll, Raccoon Camp, the bears' spring, Upfalls, the Elf Post Office, Skull Lake); Bigfoot; the yearly review and park status; marriage. Gord's meadow is the one new place, and the lab suggests it goes on land that already exists (question 1).

**Carried over.** Everything in earlier specs still holds. Phase 5 uses the jobs system for creature answers (a sixth job type, "creature", in its own colour), the clipboard sheets for the field guide, Esme's readings for hints, and the calendar on the Town sheet for full moons.

**What Phase 4's play tests taught** (Mark's tracker #1 to #65), and how Phase 5 uses it:

- **Say when something counts.** A sign that didn't count said nothing. Every creature action says what it did: "the rabbit trusts you a little more", "too close, it bolted", "that photo is in the guide".
- **Show where, don't make the player guess.** A creature's job step draws a circle on the map, like the put-down steps now do.
- **Pictures, not words.** The field guide is a sheet of pictures first: photos, sketches and silhouettes, with a few words.
- **Nothing hidden behind hover.** Choosing is by click or key, never by passing the mouse over.

## Animals

The ordinary residents from the bible: **rabbits, squirrels, hedgehogs, raccoons, badgers, deer, moose and bears.** They are part of the land, and they are what make a strange cousin noticeable.

**Where and when.** Each kind is a row of data: the ground it likes (meadow, forest, water's edge, rock), the hours it's out (day, dusk, night), its seasons, how many at once and how shy it is. Rabbits sit about in meadows by day; deer come to the tree line at dawn and dusk; raccoons and hedgehogs are night animals; bears are out in summer and autumn and asleep all winter. The bible says creatures move a little in each game, so each kind has a few home places, and the game picks which ones it uses each day.

**How they behave.** Animals wander slowly near their home place, stop to eat, look up, and rest. Each kind has a **shy distance**: come closer than that and it moves off at an easy pace, out of sight. It never runs at the ranger, and nothing is ever hurt (standing rules 5 and 7). Walking slowly (holding the walk key) halves the shy distance, and so do Lore and the cat.

**Bears.** Big and unbothered. A bear ignores the ranger unless the ranger is in its way, when it looks at them, and the ranger steps aside. No peril: just a reason to give it room.

**What they do in the park.** Visitors love seeing animals. A site with animals about earns a little more goodwill. Animals sometimes eat a garden crop (once a week at most, the same as troll badger mischief, and the cat stops it).

## Binoculars and the camera

Both are tools in the kit already listed in the bible. The binoculars are found early (a job's reward: the last ranger's pair, in the station's chest); the camera is sold at the outfitters.

**Binoculars.** Held and used (C), they show the world through two round lenses at twice the distance, with the edges darkened. Animals don't notice you. Looking at an animal for a moment **sketches** it into the field guide: a pencil drawing, where and when it was seen. Some strange cousins only show their strangeness through the binoculars (a deer whose antlers are faintly lit).

**The camera.** Held and used, a viewfinder shows what the photo will take. Pressing C takes the photo. Anything in the frame and within range goes into the guide as a **photo**, the best entry. There is no film to buy and no aiming test (standing rule 6): if it's in the frame, it's in the photo. A photo of an animal **doing something** (eating, resting, playing in the snow, looking straight at you) is marked as a good one, and a few guide pages want a good one.

**Photos of places** work too. A photo of a site at night, in fog or under the aurora goes in the guide's places page, and some badges ask for them (the bible's "night photography").

## The field guide

The guide is a sheet on the clipboard, like the Log and the Town sheet, opened with G and on the Tab ring. It has the bible's two halves and a third page:

- **Animals:** every ordinary kind.
- **Strange cousins:** every magical one, each beside the ordinary animal it's related to.
- **Places and nights:** photos of sites in special light.

**Each entry** starts as a grey silhouette with a "?", becomes a pencil sketch when seen through the binoculars, and becomes a photo when photographed. The chosen entry shows its picture large, where and when it's been seen (as small picture chips: meadow, dusk, summer), what it likes to eat if the ranger has learned it, its friendship if it's a creature, and a line or two in the ranger's voice.

**Silhouettes are hints.** An unknown strange cousin's silhouette shows on its ordinary cousin's page, with a chip for when it's out ("dusk", "full moon", "snow"). This is how the guide quietly tells the player what to look for, the way badges do.

**Filling it pays.** Every five entries the Chief writes (with a little money); finishing a half earns a badge and a Lore specialty point. Esme's readings can name a creature still missing from the guide.

## The Lore skill

The fifth skill from the bible, the same shape as the other four: experience from doing, a perk at levels 2, 3 and 4, and a choice of specialty at 5.

| Grows by | Experience |
| --- | --- |
| A new sketch | 5 |
| A new photo | 10, and 5 more for a good one |
| Feeding or a gift a creature likes | 5 |
| A creature job step | 10 |
| Settling a creature | 50 |

**Perks.** Level 2: animals' shy distance is a quarter shorter. Level 3: the guide shows where each kind lives on the map once it's been seen. Level 4: strange cousins are out a little more often.

**Specialties at level 5** (suggested names):

- **Naturalist:** animals don't move off at all while you walk slowly, and every photo of an animal doing something counts as a good one.
- **Whisperer:** creatures' friendship grows half again as fast, and a creature that has been settled leaves a small gift at the nearest cabin now and then.

## Strange cousins

Each ordinary kind may have a magical cousin that shows up among them now and then. In Phase 5, three of them, all from the bible:

| Cousin | Among | When | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jackalope | Rabbits, in meadows | Dusk, more in spring | Digs burrows where it likes, campsites included. Its answer is **coaxing it to move** |
| Magic deer | Deer, at the tree line | Dusk and night, more in winter | Antlers that glow faintly through the binoculars. Its answer is **working out what it wants** |
| Troll badgers | Badgers, near facilities | Any time, already in the park | Today they only cause mischief. Their answer is **a deal** |

**How each is settled** (the details are the lab's and kept for the player to find):

- **The jackalopes** have dug into a campground, and campers keep twisting their ankles in the holes. The ranger can't chase them (no chasing, ever); they have to be coaxed to a meadow of their own with the right food laid in a trail, over a few evenings.
- **The magic deer** keeps coming to the station's yard at night and leaving again. Something it wants is in the park, and the guide, Esme and the deer's own habits point toward it.
- **The troll badgers** will stop their mischief at one facility for a regular offering left in a particular way. The deal can be renegotiated as the park grows.

The lab adds its own hidden things here too, as the bible asks, and keeps them out of this document.

## Creature friendships and their answers

Magical creatures have friendship like the townsfolk, but **five hearts**, not ten, shown with Mark's paw hearts. Friendship grows by feeding them what they like (each creature has loves and likes, learned by trying, like gifts), by time spent near them without startling them, and by the steps of their answer.

**Answers are creature jobs.** Each creature's answer is a job of the new "creature" type, written as steps in data, with the same cards, the clipboard and the map marks as every other job. The four kinds of answer from the bible each have job steps behind them:

| Answer | Example steps |
| --- | --- |
| Befriend it | Give it something it loves; be near it at its hour three times; reach two hearts |
| Work out what it wants | Find the thing; bring it to the place; be there at the right time |
| Strike a deal | Agree terms (a choice); keep them for a week; bring the offering |
| Coax it to move | Lay a trail of food to the new place; wait for it there at dusk; clear the old place |

**When a creature is settled,** it either stays or goes, and that depends on the creature (the suggested answer to the open question). A creature that stays becomes part of the park: it's out at its hour in its place, its friendship keeps growing, and it's a sight for visitors if the ranger lets it be.

## The secret, hint or public choice

From the bible: for each magical site the ranger chooses whether to keep it **secret**, **hint** at it, or put it on the **brochure**. In Phase 5 this comes to the creatures, and the choice is made when a creature is settled, at the station's note board, and can be changed later (it moves one step at a time, so a public creature can't simply become a secret again).

| Choice | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Secret | No visitor ever sees it. Its friendship grows fastest; it may leave gifts |
| Hint | A line on the brochure ("Strange things are seen in the east meadow"). Cryptid hunters and scouts come looking; most go home happy with a blurry photo. A little income |
| Public | It's on the brochure and the map. Visitors come to see it, and the park earns from it. The creature is shyer, and its friendship grows more slowly |

Neither is wrong (the bible's words). The park's Log shows which creatures are which, and the Chief's letters react: pleased with the money, curious about what's being kept.

## Gord

The bible's were-moose: "a moose who becomes a rather awkward man at the full moon and wants help fitting in at the coffee shop." He is Phase 5's flagship, the one the "done when" means.

**Gord's meadow.** A small meadow already in the park, on land the ranger has walked (question 1), becomes Gord's. By day, all month, a moose is there: a big, gentle moose who looks a bit self-conscious. Through the binoculars something about him is off (Mark's call what: a wristwatch, a neat parting in his fur).

**Meeting him.** On a full-moon night, the moose is gone and there's a man in the meadow: tall, awkward, dressed in what he could find. He talks like a townsperson (with a portrait and moods), and he has one wish: to go into Humdrum and have a coffee at the Percolator like a normal person, just once.

**Helping him** is his creature job, over several full moons, never in one night:

1. Find him something to wear (the outfitters, or the ranger's own spare coat).
2. Learn what you say when ordering coffee (Wren can tell you; he practises on you).
3. Ask Wren to keep the Percolator open late on the next full moon (she closes at 18:00), then walk him into town.
4. Be there while he orders. It goes fine. He's thrilled.

**Afterwards** Gord stays. On every full moon he comes into town for a coffee and sits by the window, and townsfolk greet him like anyone else (standing rule 9: locals find it all ordinary). Then the ranger makes the secret, hint or public choice for his meadow. His friendship continues to five hearts, and his last heart has a small scene of its own.

**Missed it?** A full moon comes every month (standing rule 4), so a missed step waits for the next one.

## Night

Night already exists: it is dark, the lantern lights a circle and it costs energy. Phase 5 makes night a place of its own, as the bible asks ("Night is where the magic gathers").

- **Night animals:** raccoons and hedgehogs come out; rabbits and squirrels go in.
- **Night things:** fireflies over water in summer; certain forage that only opens at night (the magic ingredients already in the game become night finds); a few spots that glow faintly and give a magic ingredient.
- **Night paths:** short paths of glowing stones that are there only after dark, leading to a night-only find or a place to watch a creature. They are the first of the bible's "paths that do not exist by day".
- **It grows.** The more of the park that is open, the more night things there are (counted by sites open), so night gets wilder as the game goes on.
- **The cost stays.** Night costs energy as it does today, and late nights mean slow mornings.

## The full moon and calendar events

**The moon.** Each month (season) has one full moon, on the 14th. The Town sheet's calendar shows it with a moon, and the HUD's clock shows the moon's phase at night.

**Calendar events** are rows of data: a day, a place, what happens and how the ranger hears about it. They show on the calendar once the ranger has heard of them (from Esme, a note, the guide, or a townsperson's chat). Phase 5's:

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Every full moon | Gord in his meadow; strange cousins out more |
| First full moon of autumn | The magic deer come to the outlook, all of them, for one night |
| First snowfall | The jackalopes play in the snow (a good photo) |
| Midsummer night | Fireflies over every pond |

The lab adds a few of its own and keeps them for the player.

## Aurora nights

From the bible: rare nights when the whole park goes strange, never on the radio forecast, and hinted at by the fortune teller.

- **How often:** two or three in a year, at random, never two close together, at least one in winter.
- **The hint:** the day before, Esme's reading has a line about it ("Look up tomorrow night"). The radio says nothing.
- **What happens:** green and violet light washes over the sky and the world after dark. Every strange cousin is out. Night paths are everywhere. A photo of anything under the aurora is the guide's best kind. One aurora-only thing happens in Phase 5, kept for the player.
- **Missed it?** Another comes.

## A pet

The bible's choice of a dog or a cat.

**Choosing.** A new game asks on the first morning: the Chief's letter says a dog and a cat both need a home, and the ranger picks one and names it. In a game saved before Phase 5, the same letter arrives on the next morning.

**Living with it.** The pet lives at the station, with a water bowl to fill (as in Stardew) and a bed. Pet it each day for friendship. At the door it can be told to **come along** or **stay home**. A pet along walks beside the ranger, waits at doors in town, and sleeps by the fire at camps.

**How each helps** (from the bible, the Phase 5 parts):

| Dog | Cat |
| --- | --- |
| Sniffs out hidden finds: stops and barks once near one | Senses magic: sits and stares at where a strange cousin or magic ingredient is |
| Leads back to the trail in fog or at night: follows the way home a few steps ahead | At its best at night: sees farther in the dark than the lantern |
| Finds lost visitors: the lost-party job's circle shrinks when the dog is along | Makes shy creatures trusting: shy distances are halved |
| | Guards the garden from troll badgers, and turns up at cabins by itself |

**The other one.** The second pet can be adopted from the first winter: the other animal turns up at a cabin and stays if the ranger lets it (the open question's suggested answer). Both can live at the station; one comes along at a time.

## Badges

New badges in the bible's spirit:

- Lore at 2, 3 and 4, and the two specialties.
- Field guide: 10 entries, half the animals, all the animals, the strange cousins.
- Night photography: a photo at night, in fog and under the aurora.
- Friend of the park: a creature settled; a creature at five hearts.
- Gord's: "Coffee, black" (the night he orders).

## New data and tuning values

Every new thing is rows in tables (the bible's "Content lives in data").

| Table | Rows |
| --- | --- |
| `animals` | One per kind: ground, hours, seasons, home places, group size, shy distance, speed, what it eats, its cousin, its sprite |
| `creatures` | One per magical creature: its ordinary cousin, its hours and places, loves and likes, hearts, its answer job, stays or goes, its sprites and portraits |
| `guide_pages` | The guide's entries and their order, the places-and-nights photos |
| `calendar_events` | Day, place, what happens, how it's heard of |
| `night_things` | Night forage, glowing spots, night paths, fireflies, each with the sites-open count it needs |
| `pets` | Dog and cat: helps, sprites, bed and bowl |
| `skills`, `specialties` | Lore, its perks, Naturalist and Whisperer |
| `spoiler_creatures`, `spoiler_calendar` | The lab's hidden things, kept out of designer-facing files |

**Tuning values,** each a starting guess for the play test: shy distances per kind; the walk-slowly factor (0.5); binocular range (twice the view); camera range (6 tiles); experience per action (above); creature points per heart (100); aurora nights per year (2 to 3) and their minimum gap (20 days); the full moon's day (14); the second pet's earliest season (winter); how much night grows with each site open.

## Build order, art and tests

### Build order

**5a: Out in the woods.**

1. Animals in the world: the table, home places, wandering, eating, resting, moving off; drawn with stand-ins.
2. Binoculars and the camera: the two views, sketches and photos into the guide.
3. The field guide sheet: three pages, silhouettes, sketches, photos, chips.
4. The Lore skill: experience, perks and specialties; badges.
5. Night as its own world: night animals, night finds, night paths, growth with sites open.
6. The strange cousins appearing (not yet settled): jackalope, magic deer, troll badgers through the guide.
7. The pet: the choice, the bowl and bed, come along and stay, how each helps.

**5b: Gord and the night.**

8. Creature friendships: paw hearts, loves and likes, the creature job type.
9. The three cousins' answers: the jackalopes coaxed, the magic deer's wish, the badgers' deal.
10. The secret, hint or public choice, and visitors who come looking.
11. The moon, the calendar's events and their hints.
12. Gord: his meadow, his full moons, his job, his coffee, his choice.
13. Aurora nights and Esme's hints.
14. The second pet, the last badges, and the play test.

Each step is a patch version (0.8.1, 0.8.2 and so on) with tests and screenshots, as in Phase 4.

### Debug tools

Spawn any animal or creature in front of the ranger; jump to the next full moon; start an aurora tonight; choose or swap the pet; fill or empty the guide; set a creature's hearts; finish any step of a creature job.

### Art

Art Request 25 has every picture Phase 5 needs, in two parts to match the halves. The lab builds with stand-ins and drops each picture in as it comes, with no code change.

### Automated tests

- Each animal kind appears only on its ground, at its hours and in its seasons, and moves off inside its shy distance (and half that when walking slowly).
- Binoculars sketch, the camera photographs, a photo replaces a sketch, and a good photo is marked.
- The guide's three pages list what they should, silhouettes show their hint chips, and every five entries a letter comes.
- Lore experience, perks and specialties apply.
- Night animals and night things appear only after dark, and more with more sites open.
- The pet: the choice letter in new and old saves, the bowl, come along and stay, and each help (the dog's finds, the cat's shy distances).
- Creature hearts, gifts and the four kinds of answer step.
- Each cousin can be settled; settling records the choice; the choice changes visitors and friendship as stated.
- Gord: only at the full moon; each step waits for a full moon; the coffee scene; he comes back each month after.
- Full moons fall on the 14th; calendar events show once heard of; a missed one comes again.
- Aurora nights: never on the radio, Esme hints the day before, two or three a year, never close together.
- A Phase 4 save loads into Phase 5 with nothing lost, and gets the pet letter.

### Play checklist

For the designer's play test, as for Phase 4:

- Walk to a meadow at dusk. Watch through the binoculars, then photograph a rabbit eating.
- Open the guide (G): do the pages read as pictures first?
- Find a strange cousin from its silhouette's hint.
- Choose a pet and take it out for a day. Does it help without getting in the way?
- Stay out one night. Does night feel like somewhere different?
- Help Gord over his full moons, then make the choice for his meadow.
- Is anything about creatures unclear, slow, or wordy?
