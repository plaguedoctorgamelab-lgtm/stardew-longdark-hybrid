# Strange Ranger: Phase 4 Build Spec

Phase 4 opens the gate the other way: just outside the park lies Humdrum, a small town of people who think nothing ever happens there. It adds the town, its people, friendship, favors, shops, festivals, and the ranger station as a home. It was written before the designer's play tests of phases 2b and 3 and the jobs rebuild, and should be revised against them before the lab starts.

## Goal and scope

Phase 4 must prove that the ranger has a life outside the woods: that a day spent in town, drinking coffee, chatting, doing a favor and fixing up home, is as satisfying as a day in the backcountry, and that the two pull at each other.

The scene that proves it: the ranger wakes in a station they have made their own, picks a tomato from the garden, walks into Humdrum for a coffee, hears from the librarian that the old tower once had a keeper, takes a favor from the town board, and decides whether to head into the forest or stay for the festival tonight.

**Phase 4 is built in two halves,** as the roadmap allows for large phases.

| Half | Adds | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4a: The town | Humdrum and its map, twelve townsfolk with routines, talking, friendship, gifts and birthdays, Townsfolk Favors, the shops, clothing for warmth, named weekdays and a calendar | A player can spend a satisfying week without leaving town |
| 4b: A life there | Heart events, romance, the fortune teller, four festivals, station upgrades, decorating, and the garden | A player can make a friend, a home and a garden, and look forward to a festival |

**Not in phase 4:** marriage and a partner moving in, children, creatures (Gord included), magic nights and aurora, park status, the yearly review, tool upgrades, portraits drawn by hand if the pixel artist is not yet hired, and any new park land. Gord, though he belongs to the coffee shop's story, waits for the creature phase.

**Carried over.** Everything in earlier specs still holds. Phase 4 relies on the rebuilt jobs system: Townsfolk Favors become live as its fifth type.

## Humdrum

Humdrum is a small, tidy, slightly sleepy town right outside the park gate, a few minutes' walk from the ranger station. Its people find nothing strange about anything, and believe nothing ever happens there.

**Where it is.** Through the park gate, the road runs out of the park and down a gentle slope to Humdrum. The town is its own area, entered and left by walking through the gate, like walking into a building but outdoors. From the station door to the town square is about 20 game minutes.

**Its size.** About 90 by 60 tiles, roughly three screens by three and a half. It is meant to feel snug, the opposite of the park.

**What is in it:**

| Place | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Main Street | One street of shops, running from the park road to the square |
| The square | A green with a bandstand, the town notice board and a bench. Where festivals happen. |
| Humdrum Outfitters | Clothing, gear and seeds |
| The Percolator | The coffee shop |
| Casa Luna | The Mexican restaurant |
| Humdrum Public Library | Books, the town archive and a reading room |
| Gifts & Notions | The gift shop, which also buys what the ranger makes |
| Lindqvist Carpentry | The carpenter's workshop, where station upgrades and furniture are ordered |
| Town Hall and post office | One small building: the mayor's office on one side, the post office on the other |
| The fortune teller's caravan | A painted caravan under a willow by the creek, a little apart from the rest |
| Dusk Creek | A small creek running along the town's edge, with a footbridge |
| Houses | Eight homes, one or two for each household, each with a front door the player can knock on and enter when its owner is in |

**The town at night.** Streetlamps come on at dusk. Shops close. Windows glow. The Percolator's sign switches off at 18:00 and Casa Luna's switches on.

**Weather and seasons** reach the town exactly as they reach the park, with the town's own seasonal touches: blossom on Main Street in spring, bunting in summer, leaves in autumn, lights in winter.

**Visitors** do not come into town in phase 4.

**Rules for the cast.**

- Everyone treats the park's strangeness as perfectly ordinary, as the bible requires. Nobody is amazed by anything.
- Several of them knew the last ranger, and each remembers something a little different. None of them knows what happened. Their memories are hints only, and the spoiler addendum keeps them consistent.
- Nobody knows why the Chief never visits. A few have opinions.
- Pip is a child. Pip's friendship is that of a kid and a grown-up ranger: encouraging, funny and entirely innocent. Pip never has romance, gifts with romantic meaning, or secrets kept from family.
- Each character's likes, dislikes, birthday, routine and heart events are content data. Their heart events are in the spoiler addendum.

## Days, the calendar and routines

The town runs to a weekly rhythm, so the player learns when to find whom, as in Stardew.

### Named days and a calendar

- Each 28-day season is four weeks of named days, Monday to Sunday. Day 1 of every season is a Monday.
- The Chief's weekly budget and report, which arrived every seventh day, now arrive on Sundays.
- A **calendar** hangs on the station wall and on the town notice board. It shows the season's days, every townsperson's birthday as a small portrait or sprite head, and festival days.
- The date shown on screen now includes the day's name.

### Routines

- Every townsperson has a routine: where they are at each hour, in each season, on each day of the week, in each kind of weather.
- Routines are content data: a list of times and places for each case, with a default and exceptions. For example, Wren is behind The Percolator's counter from 5:00 to 14:00 on weekdays, walks by the creek at 15:00, and in rain stays in the shop all afternoon.
- Townsfolk walk between places along the town's paths at a natural pace. They are full characters when the ranger is in town and simple records when not, like visitors.
- On rainy days some stay in, some carry umbrellas. On festival days everyone goes to the square.
- Townsfolk never enter the park in phase 4, apart from Pip, who now and then turns up at the station's porch in the late afternoon to ask a question, and goes home before dusk; and on Pip's Ranger Test favor, when Pip walks with the ranger as far as the forest edge.

## Talking, friendship and gifts

Friendship works as it does in Stardew, made a little gentler: it can only grow.

### Hearts

- Each townsperson has a friendship of 0 to 10 hearts, 250 points to a heart.
- Romance candidates stop at 8 hearts until dating, then can reach 10. Others reach 10 freely.
- The Social page of the menu shows everyone met: their sprite or portrait, hearts, birthday, whether they've been talked to today, and gifts given this week.
- **Friendship never decays.** A friend forgotten for a season is still a friend. This differs from Stardew on purpose, as the bible's "forgiving, always" requires.

### Talking

- Talking to someone once a day gives 20 points.
- What they say depends on the season, weather, day, place, hearts, and **what is happening in the park**. They react to sites opened, badgers in the news, the first visitors, a lost party found, and new jobs finished. This is what makes the town feel connected to the park.
- Each character has at least 60 ordinary lines in phase 4, plus lines for each of those park events, and lines that change at 2, 4, 6 and 8 hearts.
- Lines are content data, chosen by conditions, never the same line twice in one week.

### Gifts

- Up to two gifts a week per person, plus one on their birthday.
- Each person has items they **love** (80 points), **like** (45), are **neutral** about (20), **dislike** (-20) or **hate** (-40). Birthday gifts count eight times. A disliked gift never takes friendship below a heart already earned.
- Their reaction tells the player roughly how it landed.
- Gift tastes are content data. Forage, cooked food, gift goods, garden crops and some odd finds all make good gifts. Each person loves at least one thing that can only be found in the park.

### Dialogue box

- As in Stardew: the speaker's name, their portrait if drawn, and the text, typed out.
- Until hand-drawn portraits exist, the box shows a large crop of the character's sprite head instead. Portraits drop in later with no code change.
- Some lines offer the player two or three replies, which change only that conversation's flavor and a few friendship points. No reply locks anything out.

## Townsfolk Favors

Townsfolk Favors become the jobs system's fifth live type, with the berry-red basket icon. Everything in the Jobs Spec applies: givers' notes, rewards shown up front, no taking on, the season's turn.

**Where they come from.**

- **The town notice board** in the square. Notes pinned by townsfolk, readable at any hour. Reading a note adds its job.
- **Asked in person.** At certain hearts or dates, a townsperson asks when talked to. Saying "I'll see what I can do" adds the job; saying "not now" leaves it on offer for a week.
- **The post office.** Now and then a letter from a townsperson arrives at the station's new mailbox.

**Rewards for favors** add one kind the earlier jobs could not give: **friendship**. Every favor gives friendship with its giver, usually 100 to 250 points, as well as its other rewards.

**Hand-written favors.** Two for each townsperson in phase 4, 24 in all, each telling a little of their story. Examples, to set the tone:

| Favor | Giver | Steps | Rewards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A Sketch of the Pond | Sol | Bring Sol a survey of the pond: be at the pond on a clear day and survey | 200 friendship with Sol · a small painting of the pond for the station wall |
| Teo's Missing Notebook | Teo | Find Teo's song notebook, dropped somewhere along the park road | 200 friendship with Teo · a recipe |
| What the Archive Doesn't Say | Agnes | Bring Agnes the last ranger's first journal page to copy | 250 friendship with Agnes · a page from the town archive · a rumor |
| Pip's Ranger Test | Pip | Walk with Pip to the forest edge and back, before dusk, and show them a survey | 150 friendship with Pip · a drawing for the notebook |

**Template favors** keep the board stocked, with a target of three on it at once:

| Template | Steps | Size | Rewards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \[Person\] Would Love Some \[forage\] | Deliver \[count\] of a seasonal forage item to them | Small | 100 friendship · $20 |
| Something Hot for \[Person\] | Deliver a cooked dish they like | Small | 120 friendship · $15 |
| A Parcel for \[Person\] | Carry a parcel from the post office to their house | Small | 80 friendship · $15 |
| \[Person\]'s Lost \[thing\] | Find a small lost item somewhere along the park road or in ring one, inside a rumor circle | Small | 150 friendship · Something more, sometimes |
| A Hand at \[shop\] | Spend an hour helping at a shop, during its hours | Small | 100 friendship · $30 |

### Gifts & Notions

Bev's shop. It sells and it buys.

- **Sells:** decorations and small furniture for the station (from 4b), wrapping for gifts, souvenirs, and bouquets (from 4b, for romance).
- **Buys:** the gift goods the ranger makes, berry preserve, garden crops and some forage, at half their kiosk price. This gives the ranger a way to turn things into money at once, while the kiosk's shelf pays more but more slowly.

### Lindqvist Carpentry

Gus's workshop. In 4b, station upgrades and furniture are ordered here. In 4a it sells planks, roofing and nails at the same prices as the catalog, but the same day, during opening hours.

### The library

Agnes's library. It sells nothing. It holds:

- **Books** on the shelves, read by interacting. Each is a few pages of park lore, local history or practical advice. Some teach a recipe the first time they are read. Ten books in phase 4.
- **The town archive**, a filing cabinet that Agnes opens for the ranger at 2 hearts. Its folders hold old park documents that hint at the park's past. Some are given as rewards.
- **The reading room,** where reading a book restores a little energy each ten minutes.

### Town Hall and post office

- The mayor is in his office on weekdays and has opinions on everything.
- The post office sends letters to townsfolk (a template favor) and handles catalog orders, which still arrive at the station next morning.
- A **mailbox** is added outside the station door. Letters from townsfolk and the Chief's letters arrive there; the note board keeps notes, rumors and jobs.

## The fortune teller

Esme Vale is the game's gentle hint system, in place of quest markers, as the bible says. She is part of 4b.

**A reading.** Once a day, during her hours, the ranger can pay $10 for a reading. Esme draws three cards from a deck of her own, and says something about each, in her own voice:

| Card | What it tells | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Sky | The weather two days from now | Always right |
| The Path | A hint toward one thing the ranger has not found: a noticed job, a magical ingredient, a find or a site's pad | Adds a large, vague rumor circle on the map, labelled with a symbol rather than words |
| The Heart | A hint about one townsperson: what they would love as a gift, or that their birthday is near, or where they will be tomorrow | Never wrong, sometimes oblique |

**Rules.**

- She never gives an exact place. Her Path circles are larger than the last ranger's rumors.
- She never hints at something the ranger cannot yet reach, such as land beyond the river.
- When nothing is left for a card, she says something kind and charges nothing for it.
- Her lines about the park's magic are written for later phases too: she is where aurora nights will be foretold in phase 5.
- She finds nothing about herself strange, and nobody in town does either.

**Friendship with Esme** works as with anyone, but her heart events are about the park and the town's past rather than about herself.

## Romance

Romance works as in Stardew, up to dating, and is entirely optional. Marriage, and a partner moving into the station, come in a later phase. This is part of 4b.

**Who.** Four adults: Wren, Teo, Nell and Sol. Any of them can be romanced by a ranger of any gender.

**How.**

1. Friendship grows as with anyone, to 8 hearts.
2. Heart events at 2, 4, 6 and 8 hearts tell their story. Each happens once, when the ranger walks into the right place at the right time once the hearts are reached. Their details are in the spoiler addendum.
3. At 8 hearts, the ranger can give them a **bouquet**, bought at Gifts & Notions for $200. If they accept, the two are **dating**, which lifts their cap to 10 hearts and changes their dialogue.
4. Dating unlocks a 10-heart event and a small daily kindness: a dating partner sometimes leaves a gift in the station's mailbox.

**Rules.**

- The ranger may date more than one person, as in Stardew. Others in town notice, and one or two say so with gentle disapproval, but there is no penalty.
- A dating partner can be told "I think we should just be friends" at any time. Hearts stay; dating ends; no hard feelings beyond one sad line.
- Nothing in romance is ever more than warm, sweet and a little funny. No content is suggestive.
- Pip is never part of romance in any way, and no romance scene involves Pip.

## Festivals

One festival each season, in the town square, on a set day. Festivals are part of 4b. As in Stardew, a festival is its own scene: on festival day the square is decorated, everyone is there, and the day is given over to it.

**How a festival day works.**

- The festival runs at a set time. The ranger can spend the day in the park and come in time, or skip it entirely.
- Entering the square during festival hours starts the festival. Time stops while the ranger is at the festival and resumes, at the festival's end time, when they leave.
- At a festival the ranger can talk to everyone (talking counts for friendship), buy from festival stalls, and take part in the festival's activity.
- A festival's activity is never a reflex test. Each is a gentle choice, a display, or a relaxed game.
- The park's visitors double on festival weekends, and the park gets busier the week before.

| Festival | Season, day | Hours | Activity | Prize |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Thaw Fair | Spring 13 | 9:00 to 14:00 | **The Grand Display:** the ranger enters one item, anything made, grown or found. Judges, led by the mayor, praise it in their own ways. | A ribbon for the station wall and $100 for the most admired entry |
| Lantern Night | Summer 20 | 19:00 to 23:00 | **The Lantern Walk:** the town walks to the creek with paper lanterns and sets them floating. Choosing a lantern's color and a wish is the activity; the wish is answered by a kind line from someone in town. | A paper lantern for the station |
| The Big Foot Supper | Autumn 18 | 17:00 to 22:00 | **The Look-Alike Contest:** the town dresses up as Bigfoot, quite seriously. The ranger judges, choosing a winner among four townsfolk. The winner's friendship rises; nobody else minds much. | The judge's sash; a long table supper that restores full energy and warmth |
| The Long Night | Winter 25 | 18:00 to midnight | **The Bonfire:** the town gathers round a bonfire in the square. Each person brings something to burn for luck; the ranger chooses what to give. Then a story from Agnes about the park, different each year. | A blanket for the station, and the story added to the notebook |

**Festival rules.**

- Everyone treats the Big Foot Supper's subject as entirely ordinary, which it is to them.
- A festival missed comes round again next year, as the bible requires.
- Each festival's lines and stalls change a little from year to year.

## Home: the station, decorating and the garden

The ranger station becomes a home the player shapes, as the Stardew farmhouse is. This is part of 4b, and follows the Interior Art Guide's rules for rooms and furniture.

### Station upgrades

Ordered from Gus at Lindqvist Carpentry. Each costs money and materials, and Gus and his crew finish it in three days, during which the station can still be slept in. Upgrades are done in order.

| Upgrade | Cost | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Shipshape | $500, 50 planks | Fixes the roof and shutters outside; the station looks cared-for, and is warmer inside: +5 warmth per ten minutes indoors |
| 2. A proper kitchen | $1,500, 100 planks, 10 window glass | A cooking range indoors that uses the cooking recipes without a fire, and a pantry chest of 24 slots that keeps food |
| 3. A second room | $4,000, 150 planks, 20 roofing | Adds a room to the station, doubling the floor space for decorating, with a second window and a reading chair that restores energy |

The station's outside picture changes with each upgrade.

### Decorating

- Furniture and decorations can be placed, moved and picked up inside the station, as in Stardew, by holding them and choosing a floor or wall spot. The Interior Art Guide's sizes and "blocks" rules decide what fits where.
- Furniture comes from Gifts & Notions, Lindqvist Carpentry, festival prizes, rewards, and some workbench recipes.
- Wallpapers and floors can be bought and applied to the whole room.
- The bed, stove, chest and workbench can be moved but never removed, so the station always works.
- Phase 4 ships about 40 pieces of furniture and decoration, and 6 wallpapers and 6 floors.
- Decorating the cabins and towers waits for phase 6.

### The garden

A small garden plot behind the station, 6 by 6 tiles, fenced, with a gate.

- **Planting:** the shovel turns a plot tile into soil. Seeds from the outfitters are planted by hand.
- **Watering:** a watering can, bought at the outfitters, waters a tile. Rain waters everything. A crop not watered does not grow that day and never dies, as the bible's forgiving rule requires.
- **Growing and picking:** each crop grows over a set number of watered days and is picked by interacting. Some regrow.
- **Seasons:** crops are seasonal. When the season turns, crops of the old season wither gently and leave clear soil.
- **Winter:** nothing grows; the garden rests under snow.
- **Uses:** crops are cooked, given as gifts, sold to Gifts & Notions, or stocked on the gift shelf.

| Season | Crops |
| --- | --- |
| Spring | Peas (regrow), radishes, rhubarb |
| Summer | Tomatoes (regrow), beans (regrow), sweetcorn |
| Autumn | Pumpkins, squash, kale |
| Winter | None |

The garden is small on purpose. Strange Ranger is not a farming game; the garden is a pleasure, not a business. Three new cooking recipes use garden crops.

## Build order, art and tests

Each half is built in steps that end with passing tests and a spoiler-free status email. The designer plays 4a before 4b begins.

### Build order

**4a: The town**

1. **The town map,** its buildings and interiors, the gate between park and town, streetlamps and seasonal touches.
2. **Named days and the calendar,** with Sunday as the Chief's day.
3. **Townsfolk and routines.** Characters walking their routines, near and far, in all weathers.
4. **Talking and friendship.** The dialogue box, conditional lines, park-event lines, hearts, gifts, birthdays, the Social page.
5. **Shops.** The shop screen, every shop, buying and selling, opening hours.
6. **Clothing.** Three slots, warmth from clothes, clothing sprite layers.
7. **Townsfolk Favors.** The town board, asking in person, the mailbox, the 24 hand-written favors, the templates.
8. **4a full pass,** then the designer's play test.

**4b: A life there**

9. **Heart events,** with a small scene system: characters walk, face, speak and pause on cue while the player watches.
10. **Romance.** Bouquets, dating, the 10-heart events, the mailbox gifts.
11. **The fortune teller.**
12. **Festivals,** one at a time, Thaw Fair first.
13. **Station upgrades and decorating.**
14. **The garden.**
15. **4b full pass.**

### Debug tools

The panel gains: go to town; set any person's hearts; give any gift; jump any person to the next point of their routine; play any heart event; start any festival; finish any upgrade; grow every crop a day; set the day of the week. Heart events and their scripts stay behind "show spoilers".

### Art

This is the most art-hungry phase yet, and characters are where image generators struggle most.

- **The town:** a town tileset (cobbles, lawns, flower beds, fences, the creek and footbridge), about ten building exteriors, the bandstand, the notice board, streetlamps, and the interiors of every shop and house, made with the Interior Art Guide.
- **The people:** twelve townsfolk in four directions with walking frames. The lab builds them first from the visitors' layered bodies, with each person's own hair, clothes and colors, so the town works without new character art. Unique sprites replace them as they are made.
- **Portraits:** 64 by 64 pixel portraits with a few expressions each are wanted for all twelve. They are the strongest case for hiring a pixel artist, and the game works without them until they arrive.
- **Clothing layers,** furniture, wallpapers and floors, crops at each growth stage, festival decorations.

The lab writes separate art requests for the town, the furniture and the garden, and a brief for the pixel artist covering characters and portraits.

### Automated tests

| Area | Must be true |
| --- | --- |
| Calendar | Days are named correctly through a whole year; the Chief's report comes on Sundays; birthdays and festivals fall on their days |
| Routines | Every townsperson is somewhere valid at every hour of every day, season and weather of a year; nobody walks through walls or stands in a doorway |
| Friendship | Talking and gifts give their points; gifts are limited as tuned; hearts never go down; romance caps hold |
| Dialogue | No line repeats within a week for the same person; park-event lines appear only after their events |
| Gifts | Every item has a reaction from every person; birthdays multiply correctly |
| Shops | Each opens and closes on time; buying and selling move money and items exactly |
| Clothing | The starting clothes give the old warmth value; each piece adds what its row says |
| Favors | Each hand-written favor can be finished and pays out; templates draw only when they can be done; the board keeps its target |
| Heart events | Each plays once, only under its conditions; none can be missed for good |
| Romance | A bouquet is accepted only at 8 hearts; dating lifts the cap; ending it keeps hearts |
| Fortune | The Sky card is always right; Path circles never point at unreachable things; nothing is charged when nothing is left |
| Festivals | Each starts and ends at its hours; time stops inside; skipping one costs nothing; it returns next year |
| Home | Upgrades apply in order and take their days; furniture never blocks the bed, stove, chest or door |
| Garden | Crops grow only on watered days and never die; seasons clear old crops; regrowing crops regrow |
| Saves | A save from before phase 4 loads; every person, heart, event, piece of furniture and crop saves and loads exactly |
| Child safety | No romance line, gift reaction or event involves Pip in any romantic way; a test lists every line and event with Pip for the designer's review |
| Performance | 60 frames per second in town with all twelve townsfolk on screen at a festival |

### Play checklist

**After 4a:**

- [ ] Walk into town on the first morning, and judge whether it feels like a different, welcoming place.
- [ ] Learn where someone will be at a given hour, and find them there.
- [ ] Hear a townsperson mention something you did in the park.
- [ ] Give a loved gift, and enjoy the reaction.
- [ ] Buy a coat, and feel the difference on a cold trip.
- [ ] Do a favor, and judge whether the friendship reward feels worth it.
- [ ] Spend a whole week in town, and judge whether it was satisfying.

**After 4b:**

- [ ] See a heart event, and judge whether it made you care about the person.
- [ ] Get a fortune reading, and follow its Path to something you hadn't found.
- [ ] Go to a festival, and judge whether you'd plan your week around the next.
- [ ] Upgrade the station and make a room your own.
- [ ] Grow something and cook with it.
- [ ] Judge whether the town and the park pull at each other in a good way.
- [ ] Write down what felt wrong, and who you wish lived in Humdrum.
