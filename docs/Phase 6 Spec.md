# Strange Ranger: Phase 6 Build Spec, The whole forest

From the lab, 27 November 2026, for the designer to change as he likes. Written from the bible, the roadmap, the open questions list and what Phases 4 and 5 taught. At the designer's request it has more sites than the bible's twelve: **eighteen new sites**, half of them mysterious or magical, plus new pieces added to five sites the park already has.

Phase 6 is when the park becomes a place you could get properly lost in. The forest reaches the Dusk River and crosses it. On the far bank lies Skull Lake, whose shape you only see once the map fills in. The seasons now change the land itself, the river and lake become roads, and at the end of winter the Chief writes the first yearly review.

## Goal and scope

**Done when** (the roadmap's test): year one can be played from the first day to the first review.

**The scene that proves it:** late in winter, the ranger sets out from the Hollow Giant before dawn and walks across the frozen Skull Lake to Nose Island. The lighthouse on Right Eye Island is still turning, though nobody lit it. The map in the pack is nearly full, and the skull is plain on the page. Back at the station a letter from the Chief is waiting.

**Phase 6 is built in three parts.** It is bigger than any phase so far, and the roadmap allows a phase to split.

| Part | Adds | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6a: The far bank | The map grows across the river to Skull Lake; seasons change the land; Troll Badger Bridge; the canoe and the cart | A player can cross the river, reach the lake, and get back faster than they went |
| 6b: Eighteen sites | The new sites in four waves, and the new pieces at old sites | Every site can be found, mapped, opened and earns its keep |
| 6c: The year | The three mysteries, furnishable cabins and towers, the yearly review, park status, badges, the lab's hidden things | Year one plays from first day to first review |

**Not in Phase 6:** the other four regions; Bigfoot's story (he can be glimpsed, never met); marriage; final sound and music (Phase 7); the "magical area" question (Phase 7).

**Carried over.** Everything in earlier specs still holds. New sites open through the jobs system's exploring jobs (find, map, join the trail, repair, sign, open), with the creature job type for any site with magic to settle. New creatures use Phase 5's friendship, paw hearts and the secret, hint or public choice.

**What Phases 4 and 5 taught,** and how Phase 6 uses it:

- **Say when something counts.** Every site step says so when it's done.
- **Show where.** Every step with a place draws its circle on the map.
- **Pace the surveying.** One or two surveys a day uncovers the area by late summer (0.8.1). The bigger map keeps that pace: the far bank has its own fire tower and outlook.
- **Pictures, not words.** Site cards on the clipboard lead with Mark's picture.

## 1. The land: the far bank and Skull Lake

**The map grows east across the Dusk River.** The forest region today runs about 960 tiles from the gate to the river. Phase 6 adds the far bank, about 550 tiles more, taking the region to roughly 1,500 by 900 tiles. That settles the open question of region size (question 2): **a long day's walk across**, as the bible asks. Gate to Skull Lake on foot is about twelve game hours, so it's an overnight trip, and the canoe makes it a day trip.

**What's on the far bank:**

- **Skull Lake,** about 360 by 300 tiles, shaped like a skull. Its islands are the features: Left Eye, Right Eye and Nose Island. A row of standing stones on the south shore forms the Teeth. The shape is never shown from the ground; it appears as the map fills in.
- **The Jaw,** the lake's long southern shore: beaches, campground and birches.
- **The north woods** between the river and the lake: old hemlock, the Hollow Giant, the fen.
- **The river's upper reach,** north of the bridge, where it falls over the Upfalls.

**The only crossing is Troll Badger Bridge,** the broken bridge the park already has. Repairing it is the first step of Phase 6. Until it's fixed, the far bank can be seen from the bank but not reached. Later the canoe and the frozen river in deep winter give other ways across.

## 2. Seasons change the land

The bible asks that a region offer four versions of itself in a year. Phase 6 makes it so:

| Season | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Spring | **The melt.** For the first week the fords run deep and can't be crossed on foot. The river runs fast: canoe trips downstream are quicker, upstream impossible. Wildflowers. Lantern Night |
| Summer | **Low water.** Sandbars and a summer ford appear on the river. Swimming at Birch Point, the crowds come, campgrounds fill. Left Eye's spring is busy with bears |
| Autumn | **Leaves down.** Easier to see far: surveys reach further. The Sprite Market is at its biggest. Raccoon Camp gets busy before winter |
| Winter | **The freeze.** From the 7th of winter, Skull Lake freezes and the islands can be walked to. Two high trails snow in and close until spring. The canoe is put away. Skating at Birch Point |

**What freezes is drawn.** The lake's ice is its own ground with its own pictures, and so are snowed-in trails (with a "closed for winter" sign the ranger can't remove).

## 3. Getting around faster

The bible's three earned ways to travel, plus walking, which always works.

**The canoe.** Built from a kit at Dusk River Landing once that site is open (planks, canvas, a paddle). The ranger paddles on any deep water: fast downstream, slow upstream, and only from spring to autumn. Put in and take out at landings, or at any bank. While paddling, the backpack has one extra row. Visitors rent canoes there too, for money.

**The cart.** The ranger utility cart is found broken down at the Old Logging Camp and repaired there. It drives only on **cart trail:** a trail widened with the shovel (a new trail job, one tile wider, a little more work). On cart trail the ranger drives at three times walking speed and carries a cart box of 12 slots. It parks where the cart trail ends.

**Something magical.** To be found, never bought, as the bible says. What it is stays the lab's secret (in the spoiler notes); it arrives in 6c.

## 4. The eighteen new sites

Every site opens the usual way (find, map, join the trail, repair, sign, open), and earns once it's open. **Magical or mysterious** sites also have something to settle, through a creature job or a mystery. Nine of the eighteen are magical or mysterious.

**Ordinary sites** (some odd, none magic):

| # | Site | Where | What it is | What it earns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The Hollow Giant** | North woods | A tree so large a past ranger built a cabin inside its trunk. The far bank's first home: sleep, save, furnish | Shelter; a second home |
| 2 | **Raccoon Camp** | North shore | An old campground where raccoons live in the tents and run it like a small town. They need convincing to share (a deal with their mayor, who wears a bottle cap) | Camping permits, once shared |
| 3 | **Left Eye Island** | The lake | A hot spring that never freezes. Bears use it as a spa and have firm views about sharing: visitors on even days, bears on odd | Spa visits (from summer); warmth for the ranger in winter |
| 4 | **Dusk River Landing** | The river, south of the bridge | A boathouse and a put-in for float trips. Where the canoe is built | Canoe rentals, float trips |
| 5 | **The Old Logging Camp** | Near the river woods | A ruined sawmill and a stretch of narrow-gauge track. The cart is here, and so is a pile of good old planks | The cart; planks; a history tour |
| 6 | **Hemlock Tower** | A rise above the lake | The far bank's fire tower. Big survey, a radio, a bunk | Shelter; the lake's forecast |
| 7 | **Birch Point** | The Jaw, west end | A picnic and swimming beach under birches. Skating in winter | Day visitors, summer swimming, winter skating |
| 8 | **Jawbone Campground** | The Jaw | A lakeside campground along the skull's jaw, with a bathroom and fire rings | Camping permits (the park's biggest) |
| 9 | **The Beaver Ponds** | Where the creek meets the river woods | A chain of beaver ponds, a boardwalk to build, and the best wildlife watching in the park | Wildlife tours; a boardwalk job |

**Magical and mysterious sites:**

| # | Site | Where | What it is | What there is to settle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | **Troll Badger Bridge** | The river | The broken bridge, mended. The badgers now charge a toll in snacks or shiny trash | A deal over the toll (their second deal; the first was 5b's) |
| 11 | **The Sprite Market** | A meadow north of the lake | A mushroom ring, empty by day. At night sprites set up stalls and trade odd goods for odd things | Earning their trust; then it's a night shop with magical ingredients |
| 12 | **The Teeth** | The south shore | A curved row of standing stones forming the skull's teeth. They hum on aurora nights | Learning their tune (ties to 5b's aurora) |
| 13 | **Right Eye Island** | The lake | A small lighthouse nobody remembers building. Its light is on every night | A mystery: the lighthouse keeper |
| 14 | **Nose Island** | The lake | A cave mouth leading down under the lake. The deepest, strangest site of the year | The end of the last ranger's trail; late in the year |
| 15 | **Upfalls** | The river's upper reach | A waterfall that runs normally by day and flows upward after dark, lifting the ranger to a ledge | What's on the ledge |
| 16 | **The Elf Post Office** | An old oak near the Hollow Giant | Tiny doors at the base of a tree | Once the elves trust you, they carry items between cabins |
| 17 | **The Ring of Nine** | A bald hilltop in the north woods | A stone circle of nine stones, one taller than the rest. On the solstice and equinox days, at dawn, the tall stone's shadow points to a different place in the park | Reading the four shadows (a calendar event each) |
| 18 | **Wisp Fen** | A small fen east of the Beaver Ponds | A boggy hollow where little lights drift at dusk. They lead lost things, and lost visitors, somewhere. Follow them and you find what was lost | Learning where they lead; then the wisps help find lost visitors |

**New pieces at old sites.** These aren't counted among the eighteen. Each is a small job at a site that's already open:

| Site | What's added |
| --- | --- |
| Kettle Pond | A fishing dock (fishing permits and rod rental) and a short boardwalk through the reeds |
| Scenic Outlook | A coin telescope. Visitors pay to look; the ranger can use it to spot a far site on the map before reaching it |
| The Old Cabin | A woodshed and two tent pads: a small campground of its own |

## 5. The sites come in waves

So the far bank opens up across the year, not all at once:

| Wave | When | Sites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | From spring, once the bridge is mended | Troll Badger Bridge, Dusk River Landing, the Old Logging Camp, the Hollow Giant, the Beaver Ponds |
| 2 | Summer | Birch Point, Jawbone Campground, Hemlock Tower, Left Eye Island (by canoe), Upfalls, Wisp Fen |
| 3 | Autumn | The Sprite Market, Raccoon Camp, the Elf Post Office, the Ring of Nine, the Teeth |
| 4 | Winter, on the ice | Right Eye Island, Nose Island |

A wave's sites can be found earlier by a player who wanders. The wave says when their jobs first reach the clipboard.

## 6. New creatures

Each follows Phase 5's pattern: paw hearts, tastes, a creature job, and the secret, hint or public choice.

| Creature | Site | Its answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The sprites | The Sprite Market | Befriending: bring odd things they'd trade for |
| The raccoons | Raccoon Camp | A deal: share the camp in exchange for the leftovers bin |
| The bears at the spring | Left Eye Island | Working out what they want: a timetable they can read |
| The elves | The Elf Post Office | Befriending: letters, small kindnesses, keeping a secret |
| The wisps | Wisp Fen | Coaxing: they move their lights where the ranger lays lanterns |

Bigfoot can be glimpsed, once, in deep winter: a shape at the tree line, footprints, and an entry in the guide marked "?". Nothing more until later.

## 7. The three mysteries

From the bible. Each is a chain of clues across the year, with notes in the Log and an end.

- **The last ranger.** Journal pages in the cabins and towers, nine of them. They read as cheerful and increasingly distracted. The last page points to Nose Island.
- **The lighthouse keeper.** Who lights the lamp on Right Eye Island, and what is it signalling to? Static Tower's second radio station, the Teeth's tune and Right Eye's logbook are its clues.
- **The wandering signs.** Trail signs the ranger puts up point somewhere else by morning. The badgers are the obvious suspects, and they didn't do it. It starts in summer, at one sign a week.

The answers are in the lab's spoiler notes and never in documents for the designer.

## 8. Cabins and towers as homes

The Old Cabin, the Hollow Giant, Static Tower and Hemlock Tower can each be furnished like the station: the same furniture, placed the same way. Each has a pantry chest and a bed. The one the ranger sleeps in most becomes their **second home**: a rescue wakes them there if it's nearest, and their pet can be left there.

## 9. The yearly review and park status

**The yearly review.** On the evening of winter 28, the Chief's letter arrives. It gives five stamps (not grades) from his rubber-stamp set:

- **Sites open**
- **Map complete**
- **Visitors happy**
- **Upkeep**
- **Wildlife and the strange**

Each is a stamp from "a start" to "outstanding". There's no failure. The review can be retried every winter 28 after.

**Park status.** One number that rises as the park grows, shown as a ribbon on the Log's front page:

| Status | Roughly when |
| --- | --- |
| Forgotten backwater | The start |
| Local secret | Four sites open |
| Weekend favourite | Eight sites open and the first campground busy |
| Regional gem | Fourteen sites open and the map nearly done |
| Famous destination | Most of the park open and a good review |

Each step brings a letter from the Chief, more visitors, and something new in Humdrum: a stall, a newcomer, or a shop's bigger stock.

## 14. Automated tests

- The far bank is reachable only over the mended bridge, by canoe, or across the frozen river in deep winter.
- The lake freezes on the 7th of winter and thaws in spring; the islands are reachable on foot only while it's frozen.
- Snowed-in trails close and reopen on their dates. Fords can't be crossed during the melt.
- The canoe only goes on deep water, and only from spring to autumn. Cart trail is the only ground the cart drives on.
- Every site can be found, mapped, opened, and earns once open.
- Each new creature can be befriended and settled.
- Each mystery can be finished from its clues.
- The review gives five stamps and can be retried. Park status rises at its thresholds.
- A 0.8.x save loads with nothing lost, and the far bank appears around it.

## 15. Play checklist

For Mark's play test:

- Mend the bridge and cross. Does the far bank feel like somewhere new?
- Paddle the river and drive the cart. Are they worth the work?
- Watch the lake freeze and walk to an island.
- Open a site from each wave. Is any too fiddly or too quick?
- Follow a mystery to its end.
- Read your first review. Does it feel fair and kind?
- Is the far bank too big, too empty, or about right?
