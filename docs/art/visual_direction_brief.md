# Strange Ranger: Visual Direction Brief, the Park Poster Look

From the lab, 17 November 2026. A plan, not a change: nothing in the game is different yet. It answers one question: can the game take on the feel of the reference pictures and the Strange Ranger Pinterest board while staying a Stardew-style pixel game, without losing what's built? It ends with everything the lab needs to start.

## Decided, 17 November 2026

The seven questions at the end are answered:

1. **Names.** The game is *Strange Ranger*; the park is **Dusk River Park**. The sign reads "Welcome to Dusk River Park".
2. **Emblem.** A **compass rose with an eye** at its centre, on a shield. It is our own design.
3. **Strangeness.** It appears **at night and around magic only**. By day the park is a cosy poster.
4. **Pixel throughout**, including the title screen and the map, with flat poster-like shading.
5. **Palette.** The lab proposes about 16 colours, with before-and-after screenshots, for approval.
6. **Fonts.** The lab chooses two free-for-commercial-use fonts and shows them for approval.
7. **References.** The 7 pictures and the 23 pins the lab could see are enough to go on.

## The short answer

Yes. Nothing already drawn gets thrown away. The pixel world stays: Mark's tiles, buildings, the ranger, the visitors and every icon. The new feel comes from five places, in this order:

1. **The title screen** becomes the park's entrance sign.
2. **The screens and HUD** become a ranger's kit: field-book paper, routed-wood signs and stitched patches.
3. **The map** becomes a vintage park poster map.
4. **Skills and finds** earn merit badges.
5. **The world's colours** shift toward the poster palette. This step is reversible and needs no redrawing.

## What the references share

From the seven pictures and the board:

- **Mid-century national park posters.** Flat, layered landscapes: mountains, then forest, then water, each a single colour. A small palette and big lettering. The Glacier map print is the model for our map: cream land, teal water, dotted trails, red stars for landmarks, a legend box and a compass rose.
- **Routed-wood park signs.** Two tones of wood, lettering cut in, a script "Welcome" over block capitals, and an emblem shield on the right.
- **Scout and adventure patches.** Circles, shields and pennants with stitched or twisted-cord edges and banner ribbons. Two or three flat colours on each, with simple pictograms: tent, axe, compass, paw, flame.
- **The strange part.** The weird merit badges (cryptozoology, curses, spirit medium), the cabin with one glowing window in a teal night, and the masked figure on the board. It's ordinary park life with something slightly off: wholesome on the surface, odd underneath. That suits a game with hidden magic.
- **The board also has** vintage ranger uniforms and portraits, flat animal sticker sheets, retro camping gear icons, topographic contour lines, and "Leave No Trace" and lake-district style labels.

**The palette in words:** cream paper, forest green, deep teal, sage, burnt orange, rust red, mustard and dark walnut brown. Muted, never neon, with warm shadows. The game's current bright cheerful greens (Art Request 06) are the main thing that would move.

## The five places, in detail

### 1. Title screen: the park entrance

- **The sign.** A routed-wood sign fills the middle of the screen, in the two-tone wood of the reference. It reads "Welcome to" in script, the park's name in block capitals, and the game's name. An emblem shield sits on the right.
- **Behind it, a poster landscape at dusk.** About four flat layers: far mountains, a ridge of pines, a lake and the near forest edge. They drift slowly for depth, with some fog and a few fireflies. A lantern on the sign post glows.
- **The menu.** New game, Continue and Quit are small planks hanging under the sign.
- **The strange touch.** Every so often, something small and odd: eyes blinking in the trees, or the lantern flickering a colour it shouldn't.

The emblem must be **our own**. The arrowhead shape and "National Park Service" are a real agency's marks, so the game shouldn't use them. Something with the same feel works: a shield holding a pine, a mountain and a lantern, or a compass rose with an eye.

### 2. Screens and HUD: the ranger's kit

- **Panels** (backpack, book, catalog, crafting) look like field-book paper: cream, with a stitched brown border and a cloth tab for each page of the book.
- **The clock and date** sit on a small wooden sign hanging in the top corner. **The wallet** is a patch with a gold coin.
- **The item bar** is a wooden rack with pegs.
- **Buttons** are plank tabs. **Hover tooltips** are paper luggage tags on a string.
- **Lettering:** one display face for signs and titles (a bold vintage slab or condensed face) and a plain, readable face for text. Both must be free for commercial use.

This changes only how things look; every screen works the same way.

### 3. The map: a park poster

Mapping is the heart of the game, so the map should be the most beautiful screen in it. It stays the same map, drawn as the Glacier print:
- cream paper with a soft edge;
- teal water and sage forest;
- dotted trails, a red star at each mapped landmark, and a small mountain at peaks;
- a compass rose, a legend box, and the park's name lettered across the bottom.

The land you haven't surveyed yet is blank paper with faint topographic lines. Surveying fills in the poster.

### 4. Merit badges

Each skill level earns an embroidered patch:
- 4 skills × 3 perk levels, so 12 patches;
- 8 more patches for the specialties.

They collect on a new **Badges** page in the book.

The strange ones: discovering a magic find, or something hidden, earns an odd merit badge in the spirit of the weird-badge picture. The badge shows something hinted, never explained. Only the count of these goes into the art request, so the spoiler list stays the lab's.

This is a small feature on top of what exists, and it gives the patch look a real job.

### 5. The world's colours

- **First, a colour grade over the world view.** The lab can do this without new art: greens pulled toward sage and teal, shadows warmed, highlights creamed. It is a single tuning setting, can be compared side by side with today's look, and can be switched off.
- **Then, if it's liked, a palette pass.** The 33 colours of Art Request 06 move to the poster palette, and every existing sheet is recoloured automatically by the art cleanup tool. Mark checks the results rather than redrawing anything.
- **Only a few hero pieces might be redrawn later,** to lean into the poster's flat shading: the station, the tower, and the cabin's glowing window at night.

## What the lab can do now, without new art

- The colour grade, with a side-by-side picture.
- The UI restyle using stand-in frames and free fonts.
- The map restyle. It's drawn in code from data the game already has, so it needs only a compass rose and landmark icons, which can be stand-ins.
- A stand-in title screen: a plain wooden sign over flat layers, built so Mark's art drops straight in.
- The Badges page, with stand-in patches.

## What the lab needs: please answer or send

**Decisions (a sentence each is enough):**
1. **The park's name** for the sign and map. For example, "Welcome to ___ National Park". Is the park called Strange Ranger, or does it have its own name?
2. **The emblem.** Which symbol goes on the shield: pine, mountain, lantern, compass, eye, a creature? Or should the lab offer three sketches to choose from?
3. **How strange.** Should the oddness show only at night and around magic, or a little everywhere, like the blinking eyes on the title screen?
4. **Pixel or smooth for the title screen and map?** The lab's suggestion: keep everything pixel art, with flat poster-like shading, so it all feels like one game.
5. **The palette.** Should the lab propose about 16 poster colours for approval, or does Mark want to pick them?
6. **Fonts.** Is the lab free to choose two free-for-commercial-use fonts and show them for approval, or does Mark have favourites?
7. **The Pinterest board.** It has 207 pins, but only 23 show without logging in, so the lab can't see most of it. Please send the 10 to 20 pins that matter most as images (a zip like the last one is perfect), and mark any that must be matched closely.

## Order of work

1. Mark answers the seven questions above.
2. The lab does the colour grade, the stand-in title, the UI restyle, the map restyle and the Badges page. All of it can be switched back. Screenshots go to Mark for a yes, no or more-like-this.
3. Art Requests 11 to 14 go out, one at a time, title screen first.
4. Mark's art drops in as it arrives. The bright look stays one setting away until the new one is approved.

Nothing in this plan changes how the game plays, except the new Badges page.
