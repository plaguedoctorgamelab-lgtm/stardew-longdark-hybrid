# Strange Ranger: Art Request 19, Humdrum

From the lab, 22 November 2026. The town works and the lab is leaving its layout alone for now, so this is the big one: everything Humdrum needs to look like a real place. Only the Percolator and Casa Luna are finished pictures today. The other fifteen buildings, the streetlamps and the notice board are flat stand-ins, and the ground is plain squares of lawn, cobbles and gravel.

Your three Stardew pictures are saved in `ref/stardew_town/` (kept out of git): a street with two shop fronts, the town hall's front, and the whole town map. As with Art Request 18 they are for **how a town reads**, not for copying. No Stardew buildings, layouts or colours are to be traced, and Humdrum keeps its own pieces: no clock-faced town hall, no fountain, no red-and-white clinic. Everything stays in our bright palette and our own style.

Pictures of the town today, by day and night, are in `docs/art/ar19_town_today/`.

## What your pictures show, and what we take from them

1. **Roofs are half the building.** Every roof is deep and textured: rows of shingles or tiles, a ridge, a chimney, moss or ivy, sometimes a dormer. From above, the roof is most of what you see. *Ours today:* flat roofs a few pixels deep. *We take:* every building gets a roof at least as tall as its front, with real texture and one distinguishing feature.
2. **Fronts are busy at the bottom.** Steps, a doormat, window boxes, a pot either side of the door, a lamp, a sign, a poster. The eye lands on the door. *Ours today:* a bare wall with a door in it. *We take:* every door gets steps and at least two things beside it, and every shop has its sign on the front.
3. **One strong colour and white trim.** Each building has one wall colour, with pale trim on windows, corners and eaves. *We take:* that, and the trim is what makes them read as built, not painted on.
4. **Paths are worn, not drawn.** The sandy paths wobble, widen at doors and junctions, and fade into the grass. The square is a paved shape with a feature in the middle. *Ours today:* straight-edged rectangles. *We take:* edge pieces so every path and paving blends into the lawn, and a centrepiece for the square.
5. **No bare lawn.** Two or three shades of grass, bushes in clumps, flowers, trees in groups, a tree with blossom here and there for colour. *Ours today:* one flat green with trees in rows. *We take:* bushes in three sizes, darker grass patches, small flowers and tufts, and two showy trees.
6. **Water has banks.** The river is a shaped line with sandy shores, a rim of darker edge, and reeds. *Ours today:* three straight stripes of blue. *We take:* bank pieces for the creek, and reeds and stones.
7. **Fences and yards say who lives where.** Each house has a fenced garden, a bed of vegetables or flowers, a bench. *We take:* picket fence, low stone wall and hedge, a gate, and garden bits.
8. **The street is furnished.** Lamps, benches, barrels, bins, a mailbox, planters, a noticeboard, a vending machine. *We take:* a set of small street things in our small-town North American style.
9. **The edge is a wall of forest.** The map ends in dense trees layered two or three deep, so you never see a straight line of trunks. *We take:* this is layout work for the lab, with the trees we already have plus the new bushes.

## Rules for every sheet

Same as Art Requests 17 and 18:

- **Style:** the bright palette, a one-pixel dark outline, and light from the top left.
- **Placement:** each piece stands on the bottom middle of its cell.
- **Size:** big drafts are fine. The lab cuts and shrinks them to the game size given.
- **Layout:** cells on an even grid. Thin grid lines between cells are fine.
- **View:** buildings in the same top-down three-quarter view as the Percolator and Casa Luna: the front wall facing us, the roof sloping up and away above it. **Please match those two**; they're the house style for the town.

Two new rules, for the buildings only:

- **Window glass is one flat pale blue,** the same blue in every building, with no reflections drawn on it. The lab swaps that one colour for warm yellow at night so the windows glow, with no night copies of each building needed.
- **The door is in the middle of the bottom edge** unless the table says otherwise, so the ranger walks straight up to it.

**Style line, at the start of every prompt:**

> True pixel art, crisp hard-edged pixels on an exact grid, every pixel a solid square, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no gradients, warm cozy 16-bit farming game style, bright cheerful palette, mid-century national park poster and scout patch feel, light from the top left.

**Town line, after the style line:**

> A small North American mountain town seen from a top-down three-quarter view: building fronts facing the viewer with deep textured roofs above them. Chunky shapes with a strong one-pixel outline, two or three tones per material, white trim, a soft contact shadow under each piece.

## What the lab will do with them

The art is only half of it. When the sheets come back, the lab will also:

- **Re-lay the ground** with the edge pieces: paths that widen at doors and corners, the square paved in flagstones with the compass mosaic in its middle, and the creek bent into a gentle curve with sandy banks.
- **Give every house a yard:** a fence or wall or hedge, a gate on the path, and a garden piece or two that fits who lives there.
- **Fill the lawns:** bushes in clumps, darker grass under trees and along fences, tufts and flowers scattered, a willow over the caravan, crabapples along Main Street.
- **Thicken the town's edge** so the forest is two or three trees deep with bushes in front.
- **Light the town at night:** window glass swapped to warm yellow, lit streetlamps, and in winter the string lights.

None of this changes where anything is, so the shops, the doors, the routines and the jobs all stay as they are.
